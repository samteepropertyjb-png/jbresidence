Buying a Condo Near JB CIQ or RTS as a Foreigner? What You Need to Check First

Sam TeeProperty Advisor · REN 80322
September 2026

A condo near Johor Bahru CIQ or the RTS Link can look convenient on a map. For a foreign buyer, convenience is only one part of the decision. Check the exact unit, the real total cost and the way you will actually use the home before paying a booking fee.

This is a buyer-education checklist, not legal or tax advice. Foreign-purchase rules, state consent and stamp-duty treatment can change. Ask your lawyer to confirm the exact position for your nationality, the project and the unit you intend to buy.

1. Confirm foreign-buyer eligibility for the exact unit

Johor has a general foreign-purchase threshold and state approval process. There can also be restrictions based on property type, location, title and the buyer's status. Some developments may have specific approvals that allow eligible foreign buyers to purchase units below Johor's general foreign-purchase threshold. The eligibility and minimum purchase price can differ by project and unit, so buyers should verify the exact unit before paying a booking fee.

Do not rely on an old advertisement, an agent's memory or a price shown for another unit. Ask for current written confirmation through the proper sales and legal process. Our wider foreign buyer guide explains why this check comes first.

2. Budget for the whole purchase, not only the property price

A purchase budget should separate these items:

Cost headingWhat to confirm
Property PriceThe agreed price, any included furnishings and the payment schedule.
State Approval / LevyWhether consent is required, the current fee or levy, and who is responsible for it.
Transfer Stamp DutyThe applicable rate and timing for the instrument. HASiL states that an 8% rate applies to transfers to non-citizens, excluding Malaysian permanent residents and foreign companies, in the stated 2026 context. Your lawyer should confirm the exact treatment before signing.
Legal / Other Applicable CostsLegal fees, loan-related charges if any, valuation, disbursements, service charges, furnishing and holding costs.

The visual above is a general decision aid. Do not treat any illustrated levy or example amount as a quote for your purchase; confirm current state approval and levy charges with your lawyer before booking.

3. Compare CIQ and RTS based on your actual travel plan

CIQ-oriented and RTS-oriented homes serve different routines. The RTS Link was officially scheduled to begin operations on 1 January 2027; treat that as a scheduled date and check its operating status again before you decide. If you cross the Causeway often, test the full door-to-door trip at the time you expect to travel. Think about lift waits, parking, walking, traffic, immigration and the last part of your journey in Singapore.

The RTS is a separate transport choice. Do not buy on the assumption that every nearby development creates the same travel advantage. Use our CIQ versus RTS comparison to frame the questions, then test the real route yourself.

4. Decide how much density and activity you want

A large city project may offer facilities, shops and more activity. A smaller development may feel calmer. Neither is automatically better. Visit at different times and look at the practical details: lifts, parking, common areas, traffic outside, noise and how many units share the facilities.

For example, a buyer considering the commercial setting around R&F Princess Cove should decide whether that mixed environment makes everyday life easier. Another buyer may prefer a different city setting or a family area outside the centre.

5. Choose the layout you will actually use

A compact unit can work well for a single buyer, a couple or an occasional Johor base. It may be less comfortable when two people work from home, need storage or plan longer family stays. A larger layout costs more, but may offer flexibility that reduces the need to move again soon.

Read 1-bedroom versus 2+1-bedroom in JB for a practical way to compare layout needs before narrowing your shortlist.

A simple pre-booking checklist

  1. Ask for written confirmation that you are eligible to buy the exact unit.
  2. Have a lawyer list all approval, levy, stamp-duty and legal costs.
  3. Test your planned CIQ or RTS route at a realistic time.
  4. Inspect the unit, tower, parking and facilities in person.
  5. Decide whether the layout fits your next few years, not only the first viewing.

Sam's practical view

CIQ and RTS proximity can be useful. They should not replace the basic checks: legal eligibility, total cost, a workable daily route and a home that fits the way you live.
