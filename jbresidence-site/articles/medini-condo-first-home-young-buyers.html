Is a Medini Condo a Good First Home for Young Buyers in 2026?

A first home does not have to be a forever home—but it should leave room for the next stage.

By Sam Tee  ·  Updated 6 September 2026  ·  8 min read

A good stepping-stone property should help you move forward, not become a loan that blocks your next home.

The first-home problem in Iskandar Puteri

Many young buyers like the landed lifestyle, but a growing number of new landed launches are close to or above RM1 million. The down payment, legal costs, renovation and instalment can make the dream home too heavy at the start of a career or marriage.

A smaller Medini condo may become a first step—if the buyer has a clear plan and does not stretch based on future hoped-for income.

The stepping-stone plan

  1. Buy a condo that fits today’s income.
  2. Live in it during the early stage of work, marriage or family life.
  3. Keep building savings instead of using every ringgit on the mortgage.
  4. When income and space needs grow, consider a landed home.
  5. Keep the condo for rent only if the demand, loan position and building rules make sense.

Why Medini may fit this plan

Current asking-price examples range from older completed units around RM400–RM500 psf to newer or compact units in the RM600-plus psf range. Choices include One Medini, Meridin Suites, Grand Medini, Elysia Park, The M @ Medini Macrolink and Bodaiju Residences. See the full Medini condo price guide.

A two-bedroom unit can provide a work room; a three-bedroom home may serve a small family longer. Compare total purchase price and monthly cost—not PSF alone.

Can the condo be rented after upgrading?

Possible demand groups include people working or studying around Iskandar Puteri, families connected to international schools, hospital visitors, Singapore-linked commuters and LEGOLAND guests. Long-term tenants value practical layouts, parking and daily convenience. Short-stay guests value capacity and easy management, but the building must permit short-term rental.

Will the first loan affect a future landed loan?

Yes. A bank will still consider the outstanding condo loan. Some documented rental income may be recognised, depending on the bank and application. Your first purchase should leave room in your debt-service capacity.

Three tests before buying

AffordabilityYou can still save after all housing costs, even without rent.
RentabilityThe layout and price match a clear local demand group.
Upgrade abilityThe loan leaves room for savings, income growth and a future purchase.

My view

A carefully selected Medini condo can be a home today and a possible rental asset later. The strategy works only when it is affordable, rentable and flexible. Compare the next step in our Medini condo vs landed guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

Must I keep the condo after buying landed?

No. Keeping, selling or refinancing are different strategies; decide using actual rental performance, loan balance, selling costs and borrowing needs.

Should I choose one bedroom or a larger unit?

Match the layout to your expected own-stay period and future tenant. Compare total price and demand, not PSF alone.
